# Boundless by CSMA: company profile

- **Date:** 2026-10-14
- **Purpose:** Understand who the company is, what it sells and to whom, so the project's problem and users are grounded in its domain (see [ADR-001](../adr/001-problem-and-idea.md)).
- **Scope:** Public information about the organisation and its products only.

## Summary

Boundless by CSMA is a members' club that gives current and former public-sector and civil-service workers discounts, leisure benefits and experiences, for an annual fee. It is a mutual organisation headquartered in Brighton, England, with a history going back to 1923.

## History

Founded in 1923 by Frank Vernon Edwards, a Ministry of Labour executive officer with an interest in motorcycle trials, as the Civil Service Motoring Club. It later became the Civil Service Motoring Association and then the CSMA Club, before the current Boundless by CSMA branding. Its roots are in motoring benefits for civil servants, and motoring and insurance remain part of the offer today.

## Who it serves

- **Members:** current and former UK public-sector and civil-service workers, including healthcare, education, emergency services, local and central government, and the armed forces. Retirees are eligible. Organisations formerly part of the Civil Service (such as Royal Mail and BT) and relatives of existing members are also eligible, per Wikipedia.
- **Tone:** friendly, warm, community-minded. Slogans include "Be More Boundless" and "The membership club for fun and new experiences", with member testimonials prominent.

## What it sells

Two annual membership tiers (prices from boundless.co.uk):

| | Boundless | Boundless Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Price | £45/year (about £3.75/month) | £78/year (about £6.50/month) |
| Positioning | Core membership | "Most comprehensive membership experience" |

**Both tiers:** Kew Gardens and Kew Wakehurst, WWT wetland centres, tastecard with Coffee Club, Kids Pass, Daily Discounts, high-street, holiday, motoring and insurance deals, member events, a membership card and mobile app, and a member magazine.

**Plus adds:** unlimited access to Historic Royal Palaces (with one child admitted per visit), the National Art Pass (free entry to many museums and galleries, 50% off major exhibitions), National Trust for Scotland unlimited access, Ramblers membership, and free Britannia Rescue roadside assistance for a nominated vehicle.

The company advertises "up to £760 in instant benefits"; this is its own marketing claim, not independently verified.

**Offer categories:** days out and attractions; dining; retail and supermarket; electronics; travel (holidays, hotels, ferries, car hire, holiday parks); motoring (including roadside assistance) and insurance; gift memberships; member events and clubs.

**Channels:** website, member login area, mobile app (App Store and Google Play), monthly newsletter.

## Sector and peers

Public-sector affinity discounts and membership clubs. Peer schemes named in a consumer roundup include Blue Light Card, CSSC, MyLifestyle and PSdiscounts. See [competitor-scan](competitor-scan.md).

## Relevance to this project

- The product is a catalogue of offers across many categories, sold on an annual fee. Members choose among offers from several partners.
- The fee (£45 or £78) is a natural reference point for a member's sense of value.
- Both points feed the problem definition in ADR-001.

## Source conflicts and limits

- **Magazine frequency:** Wikipedia says six issues a year; the Plus page says quarterly. Prefer the company's own page for current offerings.
- **Owned properties:** Wikipedia lists a motoring museum and holiday properties under the organisation, and says the Britannia Rescue breakdown service was sold in 2007, while the current site offers roadside assistance through Britannia Rescue. Current ownership and partner arrangements were not verified.
- **Tier contents:** the Plus page lists the Cotswold Motoring Museum and The Cinema Society under the standard tier only; confirm current terms before relying on either.
- Member numbers and financials were not found in the sources read.
- Company pages and Wikipedia can be out of date; figures are as of the date above.
