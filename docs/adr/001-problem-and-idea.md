# ADR-001: Problem and idea selection

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-14
- **Decision owner:** Davi

## Context

The brief asks for a small full-stack app that solves a real or realistic problem, uses AI meaningfully, and fits in 6–8 hours. It is evaluated on problem-solving, full-stack fundamentals, thoughtful AI use, code structure, ability to explain decisions, and product/UX thinking. Scope and users are mine to define.

The product domain is a public-sector membership club (Boundless-style): a catalogue of offers across many categories, sold as an annual membership.

Initial idea (A): a natural-language benefits finder. Before adopting it as is, a limited competitor scan was run ([competitor-scan](../research/competitor-scan.md)).

## Options considered

1. **A. Natural-language benefits finder.** Query → structured filters → deterministic search → explanation. In-domain, testable, bounded. Does not address whether the membership is worth its fee.
2. **A+. Finder plus a deterministic savings estimate** against the membership fee. Same core as A, one added output.
3. **B. Member support assistant (RAG over FAQs/policies).** In-domain, but a generic chatbot, and real content is not available.
4. **C. Domain-neutral tool.** Fits the brief but does not show product thinking for this domain.

## Decision

**Option A+.** The scan suggests discovery is served by conventional search and categories, and that the open questions for a member are what to use and whether the fee pays off. The estimate adds product value for little extra scope, because it is computed from catalogue fields, not generated by the model.

**In scope:** natural-language query; editable "interpreted as" filters; deterministic retrieval over a seeded catalogue; grounded one-line explanations; a savings estimate versus the £45 / £78 membership fee.

**Out of scope:** authentication, embeddings/vector search, scraping or real member data, CMS integration, payments.

## Consequences

- The catalogue is sample data, clearly labelled. Savings figures are illustrative, not claims about real offers.
- The estimate depends on assumed typical-saving values per offer; this is disclosed in the UI and README.
- If time runs short, the estimate is cut first. The core finder stands alone.

## Weakest assumption

That discovery and value-for-fee are real member problems. Evidence is one competitor homepage and one secondary roundup, with no user research. Evidence that members already find offers easily, or that the fee is not a concern, would argue for the plain finder (option A) or a different problem.
